RUBRIC 1

1. Core Questions Answered & Unit of Observation

In commercial aviation, operational delay decisions occur at two fundamentally distinct horizons. Predicting delay without clearly specifying the decision horizon leads to catastrophic target leakage or useless operational advice. This project defines and answers two distinct, calibrated questions:

STAGE 1

Departure Delay Classification

"Evaluated strictly 2 hours prior to scheduled gate departure, will the flight depart with a delay ≥ 15 minutes?"

  • Decision Horizon: T − 2 hours before scheduled pushback (CRS_DEP_TIME).
  • Operational User: Hub dispatchers, ramp managers, and pushback coordinators.
  • Actionable Lever: Taxiway queue staging, gate holding, and turnaround crew reassignment.
STAGE 2

Arrival Delay Classification

"Once a flight has pushed back with known departure delay, will it arrive at its destination gate ≥ 15 minutes late?"

  • Decision Horizon: Post-pushback / en-route (incorporates confirmed DEP_DELAY).
  • Operational User: Destination station managers, baggage crews, and transfer agents.
  • Actionable Lever: Destination gate conflict re-allocation and connecting passenger rebooking.

What Exactly Does One Row of Our Data Represent?

Each individual row in our unified modeling corpus represents exactly one scheduled commercial passenger flight leg operated by a reporting U.S. carrier between two primary U.S. hub airports on a specific calendar date.

Primary Composite Key: (FL_DATE, OP_CARRIER, OP_CARRIER_FL_NUM, ORIGIN, DEST, CRS_DEP_TIME)
Fused With: (1) Origin ASOS/METAR weather valid at T − 2 hours; (2) Incoming aircraft airframe rotational chain (prior leg delay, scheduled turnaround buffer); (3) Trailing 30-day carrier/route reliability rates; and (4) Rolling airport congestion indices.

Data Sources

Contains certified scheduled/actual gate departure and arrival timestamps, elapsed air times, aircraft tail numbers, carrier identifiers, and diversion/cancellation indicators.

Provides surface temperature, dew point, sustained wind speed, wind gusts, horizontal visibility, cloud ceiling height, and present weather codes (thunderstorms, freezing rain, fog, snow).

Data Quality Issues Encountered & Remediation Strategy

1. Flight Cancellations & Diversions (1.6%)

Issue: Cancelled and diverted flights do not have actual arrival times (recorded as NaN in BTS).
Solution: Filtered out from the training and evaluation corpus. Delay classification is statistically defined only over executed flights; cancellations are governed by safety ground-stops rather than queuing delays.

2. Irregular Weather Timestamps & Look-Ahead Bias

Issue: ASOS weather observations occur at :53 of the hour, with variable SPECI bursts during convective storms.
Solution: Fused using strict backward-as-of merging: the flight record joins to the most recent weather observation strictly ≤ T − 2 hours before scheduled departure, guaranteeing zero future weather leakage.

3. Missing Tail Rotations (12.4%)

Issue: First morning flight of an airframe, unscheduled tail swaps, or missing tail numbers have no preceding leg.
Solution: Created explicit binary indicator flags (`is_first_leg_of_day`) and imputed turnaround minutes with the domain median (55 min) within the Scikit-Learn pipeline to preserve records without row drops.

4. Class Imbalance (23.2% to 31.7% Late)

Issue: On-time flights heavily outnumber delayed flights, rendering naive accuracy deceptive (a 77% accuracy model that predicts all on-time is useless).
Solution: Evaluated models strictly on Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC) and tuned decision thresholds on validation sets for maximum F1 rather than defaulting to 0.50.

RUBRIC 3

3. Preprocessing Choices & Methodological Rationale

Machine learning models deployed in aviation frequently fail in production due to subtle training methodology flaws. Below are our five core preprocessing choices and the operational rationale behind each:

A. Strict Chronological Splitting (No Random K-Fold)

Why: Random shuffling cross-validation leaks macro-weather systems and seasonal congestion across folds. If an algorithm trains on 3:00 PM flights during a June 15 severe storm while predicting 2:00 PM flights on the same day, it achieves artificially inflated accuracy that collapses in production. Our training (2023 to Apr 2024), validation (May 2024), and testing (Jun–Jul 2024) are split strictly across time.

B. Pre-Departure vs Post-Departure Target Separation

Why: In departure delay prediction, actual pushback delay (DEP_DELAY) is the exact target outcome. Including any airborne telemetry, wheels-off time, or pushback time creates 100% target leakage. Our Pre-Departure model only ingests features known strictly at T − 2 hours. Conversely, our Arrival Delay model intentionally ingests confirmed DEP_DELAY because the flight has already departed, answering an entirely different operational question.

C. Trigonometric Cyclic Time Encodings

Why: A standard integer hour (0, 1, 2, ..., 23) introduces a severe false numerical discontinuity: 23 (11:00 PM) appears 23 units away from 0 (12:00 AM), whereas in reality they are adjacent. Transforming departure hours via sin(2πh/24) and cos(2πh/24) provides smooth continuous proximity for tree and linear models.

D. 30-Day Lagged Historical Aggregations (Burn-in Guard)

Why: Historical carrier and route reliability rates are computed over a rolling 30-day window with a 1-day exclusion lag. This prevents same-day flight outcomes from leaking into their own historical baseline. A 30-day burn-in period was enforced before computing metrics.

E. Scikit-Learn Encapsulated Pipelines

Why: All median imputers, standard scalers, and one-hot encoders are bound into an end-to-end Scikit-Learn Pipeline. Parameters were fit strictly on the training partition and evaluated identically on the test set with handle_unknown='ignore', preventing data leakage during preprocessing.

4. Baseline Architectures & Models Compared

To scientifically validate that our machine learning models generate genuine predictive signal rather than exploiting data structure, we benchmarked multiple algorithms against two domain baselines:

Baseline 1: Majority-Class Baseline

Predicts that every flight will arrive/depart on time. While achieving 68.5% raw accuracy due to the majority on-time rate, it achieves 0.000 Precision, 0.000 Recall, and 0.000 F1, and its PR-AUC equals the raw base rate (0.315).

Baseline 2: Domain Rule / Historical Rate

Departure: Historical-Rate Baseline classifies delay using the trailing 30-day late rate. (PR-AUC: 0.488).
Arrival: Departure-Delay Rule predicts arrival delay ≥ 15 minutes whenever actual departure delay ≥ 18 min. (PR-AUC: 0.911).

Candidate Supervised Learning Algorithms

MODEL 1

Logistic Regression

L2-regularized generalized linear model with balanced class weighting. Serves as our transparent, highly interpretable parametric benchmark.

MODEL 2

Random Forest

Ensemble of 200 de-correlated decision trees with balanced bootstrap subsampling. Captures non-linear thresholds and high-order feature interactions.

CHAMPION

Histogram Gradient Boosting

HistGradientBoostingClassifier (max leaves: 63, learning rate: 0.05). Selected champion due to superior PR-AUC, rapid inference, and native missing-value splitting.

Primary Evaluation Metric: Why PR-AUC Over ROC-AUC?

In flight delay prediction, on-time flights represent roughly 70% to 77% of all operations. Under this class imbalance:

  • ROC-AUC is deceptively optimistic: Its false positive rate denominator (FP + TN) is dominated by the large volume of True Negatives (TN > 50,000). Consequently, an algorithm with tens of thousands of false alarms can still report an impressive ROC-AUC of 0.85+.
  • PR-AUC is the true operational discriminator: Evaluates Precision (TP / (TP + FP)) against Recall (TP / (TP + FN)). Because True Negatives are excluded from both axes, PR-AUC penalizes every single false alarm and missed delay.
  • Brier Score: Mean squared error between predicted probabilities and binary outcomes (Mean Squared Error), measuring probabilistic calibration.
  • Bootstrap Statistical Significance (200 Resamples): We computed 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for PR-AUC gain over the baseline to prove improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.001).

Detailed Error Analysis: Why Do Errors Occur?

False Positives (False Alarms)

Predicted Late, Actually Arrived On Time:

  • In-Flight Speed Recovery: Dispatchers add 15–20 minutes of schedule padding on long-haul routes (e.g. LAX→ORD). Strong tailwinds or direct route clearances enable aircraft departing 20 min late to land on time.
  • Runway Configuration Switches: Origin ground congestion clearing faster than expected due to favourable wind shifts, releasing queued aircraft ahead of estimate.

False Negatives (Missed Delays)

Predicted On Time, Actually Arrived Late:

  • Destination Airspace Holding Stacks: Severe convective storms emerging at destination airports mid-flight, forcing en-route airborne holding or unexpected diversions.
  • Destination Gate Blockage: Flights landing precisely on schedule that remain stranded on active taxiways for 30+ minutes because their assigned gate is still occupied by a delayed departure.

Representative Error Breakdown: Airport, Hour, & Carrier Subgroups

Aggregate metrics hide critical operational differences. To provide an honest error evaluation across subgroups, we analyzed error distributions across the held-out test set (Jun–Jul 2024, 82,039 flights):

BY AIRPORT HUB

Microclimate & Runway Layouts

  • DFW (39.8% late) & CLT (39.3% late): Highest False Negative rate. Afternoon convective squalls cause sudden Ground Delay Programs (GDP) not yet visible at T-2h.
  • ORD (32.6% late): Highest False Positive rate. High taxiway queues frequently clear early when crosswind runways open, allowing queued flights to push back on time.
  • LAX (26.6% late) & SEA (26.7% late): Lowest error rate & highest precision (>54%). Stable marine layers and dual parallel runways provide predictable taxiway queues.
BY DEPARTURE HOUR

Rotational Delay Cascades

  • Early Morning (00:00–06:59, 13.7% late): Highest accuracy (92.4%). Aircraft start fresh from overnight maintenance with zero accumulated rotational delay.
  • Midday (12:00–16:59, 34.5% late): Moderate delay pressure as second/third rotation legs absorb initial hub bank snags.
  • Evening Peak (17:00–21:59, 47.6% late): Highest False Negative rate. Cascading delays from 3+ legs prior across distant airports compound into arrival hubs.
BY AIRLINE CARRIER

Turnaround Buffer Padding

  • Low-Cost Turnarounds (F9: 39.8% late, WN: 36.7% late): Tight 35–45 min turns. Gate hold snags propagate rapidly across remaining daily legs.
  • Legacy Hub Carriers (AA: 36.1% late, DL: 26.7% late): Higher schedule padding (15–25 min) on long-haul routes allows frequent in-flight speed recovery.
  • Regional Point-to-Point (AS: 26.7% late): Shorter stage lengths and dedicated West Coast terminal buffers reduce cascading failures.

RUBRIC 7

7. Operational Scope & Limitations of Findings

To maintain academic and operational integrity, the bounds of applicability of these models must be explicitly stated:

1. En-Route Convective Weather & Jet Stream Drift

METAR weather observations are captured at airport surface stations. While origin surface weather strongly influences departure queues, severe convective squall lines over flight corridors (e.g. Midwest thunderstorms) requiring tactical 100-mile diversions are not directly observed by surface METAR and can introduce unpredictable en-route delays.

2. Gate-Side Ground Operations Opacity

Pre-departure ground events—such as unexpected mechanical repair times, crew rest duty timeouts, or slow baggage loading—remain unobservable until pushback occurs. The model relies on rolling airport congestion and incoming aircraft turnaround lag as statistical proxies.

3. Non-Hub Regional Airport Generalization

The pipeline was trained and calibrated on the 10 busiest U.S. mega-hubs. While representative of high-density airspace, applying this model to small non-towered regional airports with single runways would require recalibrating airport congestion parameters.

4. Tactical Airline Schedule Padding

Airlines actively adjust scheduled elapsed flight times across seasons (block-time padding). A flight scheduled for 2 hours in winter might be padded to 2h 25m in summer, shifting the empirical threshold for what constitutes an arrival delay.

5. Reasoning About a Small Change

Hypothetical Modification: Replacing continuous departure delay in minutes (DEP_DELAY) with a binary indicator (IS_DEP_DELAYED $\ge 15\text{m}$).

Collapsing delay into binary destroys this non-linear interaction with route elapsed time.
